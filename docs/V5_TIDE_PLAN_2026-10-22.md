# Coast Camp — the tide: our own way to open the board (plan, 2026-10-22)

**Status:** approved as a direction ("Zuvshuurnu, tuluvluguugee bich"); this plan is
for the user's approval before code.
**Why:** the user, playing build 3: the board opens too easily ("merge hiinguut amarhan
zadraad board neegdeed baina"), and Coast Camp must not copy other games ("Busdiin
togloomiig shuud huulj bolohgui"). Two current features are too close to specific
games: digging sand by merging next to it, and numbered cells that open by level.
Genre conventions (merging, generators, energy, orders, levels) stay; the board's
opening, the boxes and their names become Vaultwake's own, built on the Tideglass theme.

## 1. The tide replaces sand and numbered cells
- At the start only the camp is dry: Rivet Rover and the eight cells around it. Every
  other cell is **under the tide**. Items lying on the sea floor show faintly through
  the water (parts, crates, energy pools), so the player sees what is waiting.
- **Each restoration on the map lowers the tide one step.** The water draws back from
  the camp in a band, and that band becomes dry board; whatever lay under it is now on
  the board. Merging no longer opens anything.
- Landing Beach has seven restorations, so seven tide steps, by distance from the camp
  (row + column, 7×7):

  | Restoration | Cells that dry | New cells |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 Landing path | distance 3–4 | 6 |
  | 2 Camp tent | 5 | 6 |
  | 3 Signal mast | 6 | 7 |
  | 4 Workshop | 7 | 6 |
  | 5 Supply pier | 8 | 5 |
  | 6 Beacon | 9–10 | 7 |
  | 7 Cliff path | 11–12 | 3 |

  Generators stand above the water from the start (the Survey Kite still needs the
  signal mast; the Echo Crucible waits for the next area).
- A restoration now does two visible things at once: the place appears on the coast
  picture, and the tide pulls back on the board. Board space becomes the reward for
  progress instead of a side effect of merging.
- Water cells cannot hold items, are not merged or moved, and show in the selection
  panel what lies there and which restoration uncovers it.
- Levels keep their chest (energy and a crate); they no longer open cells.

## 2. Crates the tide brings (our own boxes)
- Generator boxes become **flotsam**: crates the sea washes in. Names and look are new:
  **Driftwood crate → Sea chest → Captain's trunk** (was Small/Medium/Large box), with
  a rope-tied crate, an iron-banded chest and a brass trunk drawn for Coast Camp.
- Rules stay as now: tap twice for a free part of the crate's generator chains; two
  unopened twins merge into the next crate; a Captain's trunk fed to its generator
  raises the generator's level.
- Where crates come from: some lie under the tide and wash up as it recedes; level
  chests; every third crew order.

## 3. Smaller consequences
- The policy and pacing targets stay: first restoration within about a minute, Landing
  Beach in 15–25 minutes for the scripted player, energy low by the end. Because the
  camp starts with nine cells and only restorations add more, the first two
  restorations will be cheap enough to come before the camp feels cramped; costs are
  tuned with the simulation.
- New chains (rope, shells) stay tied to generator levels; whether they open earlier
  is a separate decision.

## 4. Saves
- Coast Camp save version 2. A version-1 save loads with its sand and numbered cells
  turned to water at the right tide step (cells of places already restored are dry;
  what was buried stays under the water), its boxes renamed by size only (same data),
  and nothing lost. A test loads a version-1 save. Classic V5 saves are untouched.
